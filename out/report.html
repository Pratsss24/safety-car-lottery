Finding them, and knowing what you found

A safety car is obvious in lap times: everybody is thirty to sixty per cent slower at once. That part is four lines of code. The hard part is that rain does the same thing, and a detector that does not ask why will price a lottery that never happened.

The gaps separate them. Under a safety car the field is bunched up deliberately and the spread of the cars collapses, typically to a fifth. Weather does the opposite, because drivers differ far more in the wet. Same lap-time signature, opposite effect on the order.

A check that uses no lap times

A safety car makes a pit stop cheap, so if these laps really are safety cars the stops should pile up on them. 20.2% of the season's 912 pit stops happened on 4.7% of its race-laps, an enrichment of 4.31 times. Nothing downstream depends on this number; it is here so the detector can be wrong in public.

A stop sheltered on both laps costs 5.2 s against 23.2 s under green: a prize of 18.0 s, about 78% of a pit stop. But a stop with only its in-lap sheltered costs 31.5 s — more than a green one. The car dives in behind the safety car and rejoins just as the field is released, paying the pit lane's price and collecting none of the discount.

Who was in a position to use it

Over 18 neutralisations, 160 of 280 cars that still had a stop to make took it there (57%). The rest were already on their last set and watched their rivals buy the same stop for a fifth of the price. Which group a car was in depended on where it happened to be in its stint when somebody else crashed.

What this does not do

It does not know where the safety cars actually were — it infers them, and the three-way split leaves five periods unclear rather than forcing them. It cannot separate a full safety car from a virtual one, which have different slowdowns and different prizes. It prices a stop against staying out, which is not the same as the positions gained, because that depends on who else stopped. And it is one season: eighteen neutralisations is why the interval on the prize is as wide as it is.
